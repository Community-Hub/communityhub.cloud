## Evidence-ranked gaps in the rejected build

### 1. The homepage still contained unreconciled extra chapters

**Evidence strength: high; direct source observation, with strong narrative implications.**

The homepage assembly still appended `Happening now` and a second `How the Hub works` chapter after Engage/Educate/Motivate. The former contained two twelve-event lists plus partner-calendar screenshots. The latter retained the older `Data in → Hub → Out to people` formulation, including orbs among the outputs. The assembly was `hero + people + why + engage + products + live_now + hub`.

This repeated Calendar and Data Hub material after those subjects had already been introduced. It also retained a simplified conceptual explanation on the homepage while the products overview had received a different, source-based model. The narrative had not been reconciled as a whole.

**Transcript:** **11:56:29–11:58:35** specifies the concise overview and its purpose; **12:10:04–12:12:38** rejects the confusing simplified conceptual model and asks for an explanation closer to the presentation reference. Frames **2195/2360** show the criticized model; **2290/2390** show the intended presentation model.

**Interpretation boundary:** John did not explicitly name and order deletion of these two later homepage sections. The finding is that their continued inclusion needed an editorial justification against his stated structure, rather than being inherited automatically from the prototype.

### 2. Product chapters still asked visitors to operate nested interfaces

**Evidence strength: high for visible complexity; the design consequence is an interpretation.**

In `home-educate-1280.png`, the product chapter has four product tabs, then four visualization tabs, a partner link, product/demo links, a chart link, and a help link. In `home-engage-390.png`, visitors see media-carousel arrows and progress bars, separate product-rail arrows with `1 / 3`, and a header down arrow. `home-motivate-1280.png` combines Calendar/Voices selection with a second row of four calendar-source choices.

The visitor has to understand several different meanings of “next” and decide which local interface to operate. This makes the homepage feel like a collection of small applications rather than a guided introduction to the products. The retained live data is useful, but its presentation brings detailed controls into the executive summary.

**Transcript:** **11:44:07–11:44:42** links grouping to understanding one part of the story; **11:57:16–11:58:35** defines the overview's role; **12:00:37–12:02:22** asks for a clearer path and compact components within each theme; **12:16:25–12:16:35** asks for less complexity.

**Interpretation boundary:** The meeting does not prohibit tabs, interactive previews, or multiple useful links. The issue is their combined hierarchy and cognitive load in these specific compositions.

### 3. Restored product wording was not consistently paired with the right explanatory visual

**Evidence strength: high.**

The default Building Dashboard view in `home-educate-1280.png` and `home-educate-390.png` illustrates its building-specific paragraph with a heatmap captioned **“Whole city electricity, last 60 days.”** A separate Building tab exists inside that preview, so the visitor must change the demonstration to see the most directly relevant illustration.

Original frame **1715** shows the source website pairing Building Dashboard with a recognizable building dashboard, Citywide Dashboard with its animated community landscape, and Data Hub with its own tools. Those pairings help explain the distinction between products before a visitor interacts.

**Transcript:** **11:57:16–11:58:35** asks the overview to explain approaches and connect visitors to each product; **11:59:09–12:02:22** asks for clearer grouping and guidance.

This is a content/comprehension mismatch, not a reason to discard the working live visualization. The correct introductory illustration and the deeper interactive demonstration serve different purposes.

These compositions give considerable emphasis to reusable containers and interface furniture. The original source's strongest material is specific: people, actual public screens, identifiable places, and recognizable product imagery. The current testimonial view had moved closer to that material, while several other views remained dominated by component patterns.

**Transcript:** **11:38:26–11:40:33** favors complete, substantial photos and people-focused examples; **12:08:30–12:09:30** objects to the large containing structure and formatting; **12:15:29–12:16:35** asks for simplicity and directness.

**Interpretation boundary:** This is not a universal prohibition on cards, rounded corners, borders, or whitespace. The product overview's useful direct links and equal sizing respond to real requirements. Replacing every card indiscriminately would be another unsupported design rule.
